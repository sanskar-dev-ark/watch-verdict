What sits behind Moctale

Most of Moctale needs an account. Profiles, person pages and the Explore pages all redirect to a login page that search engines are told not to index. Only the home page and title pages are public.

Built for search and sharing

Title pages use readable addresses such as /content/carrie-2026, with matching patterns for people (/person/mike-flanagan) and browsing (/explore/country/usa, /explore/language/english). Each title page ships its own search description, cast list and poster preview for social sharing, and marks itself as a TV show or film. The page code shows a Next.js site rendered on the server.

Reviews are the sign-up hook

Logged-out visitors see the meter and four sample reviews. The sample reviews use the same names, wording and like counts on every title, so they are placeholders, not real reviews. Real reviews sit behind "Create an account to see reviews".

Login is guarded

Sign-in takes a username or a phone number, has a "Forgot password" flow, and runs a Cloudflare bot check before submitting.

They host their own images

Posters and banners come from Moctale's own image server, not from TMDB. That lets them use custom artwork for featured sections and stops them depending on another service's image links.

Home sections are hand-picked and commercial

Talk Of The Town mixes films, shows and trailer drops with labels such as New Trailer, Teaser Trailer, New Movie, New Show and Encore Re-release. "Watch It With District" promotes a cinema-ticket partner. There is an Editor's Pick of the Week, and per-service sections ("Don't Miss These on Netflix", JioHotstar, Prime, Crunchyroll), each with its own banner image. There is an ad slot on every page, app-store buttons, and an "Advertise with Us" page.

Influencer distribution

The three founders are film YouTubers with several million subscribers between them, and each has a Moctale profile linked from the About page. Community channels on Discord, WhatsApp, Instagram and Reddit come straight from those audiences.

The meter works differently

Moctale's headline figure is the share of the single most common verdict. Ours is the share who said Go for it or Perfection. The same votes on Carrie give very different headlines:

Moctale meter
71%
800 of 1,128 votes were Go for it, the leading verdict
Telly Verdict meter
96%
Go for it 71% + Perfection 25%

Our version answers "should I watch this?" more directly, but it bunches well-liked titles near 100%. Moctale's tells you the mood but can drop when votes split between Go for it and Perfection, which reads oddly for a great title. Either works. We should pick one on purpose and explain it on the page.

What Moctale users say

They like

  • The four simple verdicts and the meter, which several users call the one thing that sets Moctale apart.
  • A smooth interface that felt polished on day one.

They complain about

  • Too much mixed together: Reddit-style threads, Discord-style chat and Letterboxd-style logging in one app.
  • Verdict-only "blank" reviews sitting among written ones. Users want them separated.
  • No way to share a rating as an Instagram or Snapchat story card.
  • Some want games covered too.

Paraphrased from public posts in r/moctale and r/pj_explained. We can't read full threads from here, so this list covers the main themes, not every comment.

What to build next

P1

Search-friendly title pages

Serve each title, person and calendar week as real HTML with readable addresses (/film/paddington-in-peru-2024), page titles, descriptions and social preview images. Without this, search engines can't index us, and that's where most visitors to a site like this will come from.

P1

Real accounts

Move to Postgres (Supabase), add email verification, password reset, a bot check on sign-up and login, and rate limits. Sign-in with Google and Apple will help sign-up rates.

P1

Pick the meter maths

Keep "% Go for it or Perfection", or switch to Moctale's "% of top verdict", and explain it in one line under the meter.

P2

UK cinema tickets

Add "Book tickets" on films in cinemas, linking to Odeon, Cineworld, Vue, Everyman and Picturehouse through an affiliate network. This mirrors Moctale's District section and is our clearest first source of revenue.

P2

Share cards

Generate a portrait image with poster, verdict and score for Instagram and Snapchat stories. Moctale users are asking for this and Moctale doesn't have it yet.

P2

Trailer drops and hand-picked home sections

Label new trailers and teasers on home, and let editors set a section's banner image and order alongside the automatic sections.

P2

Notifications

Tell people when someone likes or replies to their review, follows them, or when a title on their Watch later list arrives on a UK service or in cinemas.

P3

Phone app and own images

Start with an installable web app (home-screen icon, offline shell), then native apps once there's traction. Cache posters on our own storage.

Where we're already ahead

  • Built around the UK: BBFC certificates, UK cinema and streaming dates, and UK services including BBC iPlayer, ITVX, Channel 4 and NOW.
  • A public release calendar covering cinemas, streaming and UK TV channels in one view.
  • Scores for six parts of a film alongside the four verdicts.
  • A discussion thread on every title, instead of sending people off to Discord.
  • Points, levels, badges and leaderboards that reward useful reviews.
  • Fully public reviews, which help search engines once pages are indexable.

How this was done

We studied only what any visitor can see: public pages, their page metadata, and public posts. We did not log in to Moctale, call its private APIs, or inspect its apps, so features behind login are inferred from Moctale's own About page and user posts and marked as such. The Telly Verdict side reflects the local build, tested against a stand-in for TMDB because the TMDB key isn't set up yet.
